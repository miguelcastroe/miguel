MouthPad^

Work / Innovation

A hands-free interface controlled by the tongue.

The overlooked opportunity

People with limited upper-limb function can struggle with traditional input devices such as keyboards, mice and touchscreens. Even as assistive technologies advanced, interaction could still be low-bandwidth.

The tongue offered a different possibility. Its network of muscles and nerves gives it the dexterity and sensitivity to generate a wide range of precise movements.

The idea

Turn that dexterity into an interface. MouthPad^ is a custom oral device that sits against the roof of the mouth and translates tongue movement into hands-free control of computers, smartphones, tablets and other electronics.

The system enables eight dimensions of movement, including jaw, head and sip-puff gestures, creating a more seamless and intuitive alternative to bulky or conspicuous assistive devices.

How it works

Each MouthPad^ begins with an intraoral 3D scan so the splint can fit the user’s mouth. A biocompatible resin forms the device and shields the electronics inside.

A flexible circuit board contains sensors, a processing unit and a transmitter. A water-tight sealing process protects the electronics from saliva, while wireless software updates allow the device to keep evolving.

Built with the community

MouthPad^ was developed with input from the disability community and guided by principles of universal design and inclusive technology. The aim was to give people with limited upper-limb control a more independent way to perform everyday digital tasks.
